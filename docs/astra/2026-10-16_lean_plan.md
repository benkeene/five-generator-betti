The cutoff proof is dimension-independent. If a coordinate of z exceeds `maxGen`, choose a positive coordinate j of y and replace `n_j e_i` inside z by `n_i e_j`. The resulting factorization shares a positive coordinate with both z and y. Consequently disconnected fibers have all coordinates bounded by `maxGen`, giving the existing cutoff.

No assumption `2 ≤ e`, generator ordering, gcd, or minimality is needed. Even `e = 0` works.

**Reduction details that must be retained**

After extracting `a`, first handle `c²`, `bd`, and `bᵏ`; each produces positive `a`. In the remaining case, write the exponents of `b,d,e` as `B,t,j`.

- If `B>0`, then `d=0` and `B<k`.
- Excess `bᴮceʲ` uses H’s first indexed relation with index `k−1−B`; it introduces `aᴮ`.
- Excess `bᴮeʲ` uses the second indexed relation with that index; it introduces `a^(B−1)`. The sole exception is `B=1`, giving `b e^(k−1) → c d^(k−1)`, after which `b=0` and `d<k`.
- With `b=0`, applying `dᵏ → c e^(k−1)` either makes `c²` immediately, or leaves `c=1`; a second application makes `c²`. Thus no unproved termination claim remains.
- Excess `dᵗeʲ`, `t<k`, uses second N index `s=k−1−t`. Its left e-exponent is `q+1−t`; its right a-exponent is `q+2−k+t≥1`.
- Excess `cdᵗeʲ` uses first N index `r=k−1−t`. Its left e-exponent is `q−t`; its right a-exponent is `q+3−k+t≥2`. At the boundary `q=t=k−1`, the left e-exponent is exactly zero.

These finite cases establish `residual_step`; strong induction on the residual weight establishes the main normal-form theorem. Induction on the preserved whole weight would be invalid.

**Minimality risks**

1. **H irredundancy must be proved for its actual generating list.** Degree two separates the two quadratics. In ordinary degree `k`, kill quadratic consequences with coefficient selectors at monomials outside `(a,c²,bd)`. The surviving degree-`k` supports are pairwise disjoint; this includes both exceptional surviving right sides `ce^(k−1)` and `cd^(k−1)`. Formalizing coefficient selectors avoids constructing an unnecessary quotient-ring basis.

2. **Weighted projection needs explicit coefficient support control.** An assertion that “only relevant degrees survive” is not a Lean proof. Stream A should expose a finite coefficient representation with homogeneous coefficients or equivalent support restrictions. Positive generator weights ensure no nonconstant monomial has weight below `m`.

3. **N minimality is characteristic-independent.** After projection, every other N disappears because its distinct degree differs by less than `m`. Applying ψ kills H, whereas
   \[
   \psi(N_r)=s^{q+1}t^r(t^m-s)\ne0.
   \]
   Prove nonvanishing by distinct monomials/coefficient extraction; do not depend on characteristic zero.

4. **Boundary H collisions are real.** At `q=k−1`, the only degree-`k` H relations at or above `D` are `b e^(k−1)−c d^(k−1)` at `D`, and `dᵏ−ce^(k−1)` at `D+k−1`. More generally, `H_degree_lt_D_add_m` ensures any N multiplier surviving projection is scalar. At most one N has the matching degree; ψ forces its scalar to vanish. Then H-only nonredundancy applies.

5. **Natural subtraction requires bounds before algebra.** The exact N expressions `q+1+r−k` and `q+2+s−k` are mandatory. Prove the required inequalities with `omega`, rewrite truncated subtraction using the bounds, then use `ring`/`omega`. The expression `q−k+1+r` is wrong at the boundary.

6. **Apéry must avoid unguarded subtraction.** The supplied definition says there is no attainable `t` with `d=t+m`. Defining it merely by nonattainability of `d-m` incorrectly rejects all attainable `d<m`, because truncated subtraction gives zero.

10. For remaining \(cd^te^j\), excessive length uses first N index `r=k-1-t`.

The last two replacements have:

| Case | N left e-exponent | N right a-exponent |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(d^te^j\) | \(q+1-t\) | \(q+2-k+t\ge1\) |
| \(cd^te^j\) | \(q-t\) | \(q+3-k+t\ge2\) |

At `q=t=k-1`, the second row’s left e-exponent is zero.

Thus there are at most two rewrites introducing no a before reaching a normal form or a positive a-extraction. This proves termination of the staged lemma without asserting that the preserved whole weight decreases.

- If `z 0>0`, extract its a-power and apply induction to the residual.
- Otherwise use `residual_step`.
- A positive extracted a-power makes the residual weight strictly smaller.

The proof has three parts.

**H within H.** Apply ordinary-degree projection, using weights constantly one.

- In degree two, coefficient extraction separates `c²-bd` and `bd-ae`.
- In degree k, higher H relations have scalar multipliers. Quadratic consequences vanish under coefficient selectors at monomials outside `(a,c²,bd)`.
- The surviving supports of the degree-k relations are pairwise disjoint.

Implement those coefficient selectors directly. A quotient-ring basis for `K[b,c,d,e]/(c²,bd)` is unnecessary.

**N within the full list.** Project to degree \(D+r\). For another N relation:

- if its degree exceeds the target, its coefficient is zero;
- if its degree is smaller, the required positive multiplier degree is below m, hence zero;
- equal degree forces the same N index.

After deleting the target N, all N terms disappear. Apply ψ: H maps to zero, whereas the target maps to
\[
s^{q+1}t^r(t^m-s)\ne0.
\]

Prove nonvanishing by distinct monomials or coefficient extraction. It is valid in every characteristic.

**H within the full list.** First project by semigroup weight. The bound `H_degree_lt_D_add_m` implies that any surviving N multiplier must be scalar. Distinct N degrees permit at most one matching N. Applying ψ forces that scalar to vanish. Now invoke H-only nonredundancy.

This order matters: **ordinary-degree projection is not initially applicable to the full H∪N list**, because N is not ordinary-homogeneous.

At `q=k-1`, the matching cases are:

| H relation | Degree |
|---|---|
| \(be^{k-1}-cd^{k-1}\) | \(D\) |
| \(d^k-ce^{k-1}\) | \(D+k-1\) |

The first has H index `.inr (.inr ⟨k-2, …⟩)`; the second has H index `.inl 3`.

The universal bound below `D+m` suffices for the proof. The explicit collision theorem should still be proved as a boundary regression.
